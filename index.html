About the Project

Project Overview

Music popularity depends on many factors, from musical characteristics to artist exposure and changing listening behavior. This project investigates whether measurable Spotify audio characteristics can help explain and predict song popularity using a historical dataset spanning releases from 1921 to 2020.

The analysis combines exploratory data analysis, correlation analysis, multicollinearity assessment, regression modeling, ensemble learning, model diagnostics, and feature importance analysis.

Research Question How well do Spotify audio features explain and predict song popularity, and how much additional predictive information is provided by release year?
Dataset

Historical Spotify Data

The dataset contains 170,653 Spotify tracks and 19 variables. The modeling analysis focuses on nine numerical audio features together with release year.

🎧

Audio Characteristics

Acousticness, danceability, energy, instrumentalness, liveness, loudness, speechiness, tempo, and valence.

⭐

Target Variable

Spotify popularity score ranging from 0 to 100. The mean popularity in the dataset is approximately 31.43 and the median is 33.

📅

Time Period

Tracks span from 1921 to 2020, making it possible to investigate long-term temporal patterns in popularity.

Methodology

Analysis Approach

🧹

Data Quality & EDA

Dataset structure, missing values, duplicate records, descriptive statistics, unusual values, distributions, and temporal patterns were examined before predictive modeling.

📊

Statistical Analysis

Pearson correlations, year-adjusted correlations, predictor correlations, and Variance Inflation Factors were used to understand relationships and potential multicollinearity.

🤖

Predictive Modeling

Linear Regression, Random Forest, and Histogram Gradient Boosting were compared using audio-only and audio-plus-year specifications.

Release year shows a strong positive association with popularity. More recently released tracks tend to have considerably higher popularity scores in this historical dataset.

1921 → 2020
Strong Temporal Pattern

Average and median popularity increase substantially across release years, indicating that temporal structure is central to how popularity appears in this dataset.

Audio Features

Raw Relationships with Popularity

Several audio features initially appear moderately associated with popularity. However, these relationships weaken substantially after accounting for release year.

Acousticness r = -0.573
Energy r = 0.485
Loudness r = 0.457
Instrumentalness r = -0.297
Danceability r = 0.200
Much Weaker After Year Adjustment
Temporal Confounding

After removing the linear effect of release year, the audio-feature correlations shrink substantially. For example, energy falls from approximately 0.485 to 0.055, loudness from 0.457 to 0.072, and acousticness from -0.573 to -0.086. This suggests that much of the raw relationship between audio characteristics and popularity is linked to historical change.

Model Interpretation

Random Forest Feature Importance

84.7%
Release Year Importance

Release year dominates the selected Random Forest model, accounting for approximately 84.7% of its impurity-based feature importance.

Individual audio features each account for a much smaller share. Acousticness is the largest audio feature at approximately 1.9%, followed closely by loudness, instrumentalness, tempo, and danceability.

This reinforces the conclusion that the model's strong random test-set performance is driven primarily by temporal information, rather than audio characteristics alone.

Insights

Main Findings

📅 Release Year Is the Dominant Predictor

Popularity rises strongly across release years in this dataset. Release year correlates with popularity at approximately 0.862 and dominates the selected Random Forest's feature importance.

🎵 Audio Features Still Carry Predictive Information

Random Forest using only audio characteristics reaches R² = 0.585, showing that musical characteristics contain meaningful information even without release year.

🧠 Non-Linear Models Improve Audio-Only Prediction

Random Forest and Histogram Gradient Boosting both outperform audio-only Linear Regression, indicating that non-linear relationships and feature interactions are useful.

⏳ Raw Audio Correlations Are Strongly Time-Dependent

Energy, loudness, and acousticness initially show substantial correlations with popularity, but these relationships become weak after adjusting for release year.

⚠️ High R² Does Not Mean Audio Alone Explains Popularity

The strongest model includes release year, so its 0.810 R² should not be interpreted as evidence that musical characteristics alone explain 81% of popularity variation.

Critical Evaluation

Limitations

Random Train-Test Split

Tracks from similar historical periods can appear in both training and test sets. A time-based validation strategy would better assess genuine prediction of future releases.

Popularity Is Time-Structured

Release year receives approximately 84.7% of the Random Forest feature importance, suggesting that much of the model's predictive strength reflects temporal patterns.

Zero-Popularity Tracks

More than 27,000 tracks have a popularity score of zero, which may represent genuinely unpopular songs, catalog effects, or measurement limitations.

Missing External Factors

Artist reputation, marketing exposure, playlist placement, label support, collaborations, genre, and social-media attention are not included in the current feature set.
